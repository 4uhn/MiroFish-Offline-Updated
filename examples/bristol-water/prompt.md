Simulate public reaction on social media over the first week of the April 2026 Bristol water contamination crisis. Bristol Water has issued a "Do Not Drink" notice for about 45,000 households in the BS5, BS6 and BS7 postcodes (Easton, St George, Redland, Cotham, Montpelier, Bishopston, Horfield, Lockleaze). Lead has been measured at three times the legal limit, along with the solvent trichloroethylene, in water leaving the Purdown treatment works. The source is a breached containment cell at the former Eastville Chemical Works site, which was disturbed by Persimmon Homes groundwork. Bristol City Council has declared a Major Incident, schools are closing and bottled water is being handed out at eight distribution points. NHS Bristol is offering blood lead tests to children under 5 and pregnant women, and 12 young children have elevated blood lead levels. The Drinking Water Inspectorate has issued an enforcement notice, and the Secretary of State has ordered an independent review.

Model how residents, parents, health workers, journalists, local campaigners and the institutions involved (Bristol Water, the council, NHS Bristol, the Environment Agency, the DWI, Persimmon Homes) react, share information and rumours, and assign blame. Key things to observe:
- Whether anger focuses on Bristol Water, Persimmon Homes, the regulators or the council.
- Whether the campaigners' 2019 warnings about the site gain traction.
- How health anxiety about lead in children spreads compared with official reassurance.
- How trust in official updates changes as the enforcement notice, the blood test results and the apology arrive.
